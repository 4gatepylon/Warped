What you're seeing

  1. Each wormhole has two mouths. From outside, a mouth bends light just like a black hole of the same size: straight lines curve around it and there's a bright Einstein ring. But where a black hole's shadow would be black, you see the world around the other mouth. That light came through the throat.
  2. Go through. From the start, the mouth ahead leads to the house door: press F (or hold W). You fly along the same bent paths light takes, so whatever is in the middle of your screen is where you end up. You come out of the other mouth facing the way the middle of the disk showed. The throat magnifies small aiming errors a lot, so G lines you up exactly.
  3. The mouth on the left leads to a spot high above the plaza, looking down. Look into it to see the plaza, and the first wormhole, from above.
  4. Go in off-centre or from the side and you come out tilted: the throat is a sphere, not a flat doorway. You level out after a moment.
  5. Shoot the laser into a mouth and watch it come out of the other one. Leave an emitter and fly round to see the beam's two halves.
  6. Throw a ball (T) into a mouth from a few blocks away. It speeds up as it falls in, comes out of the other mouth as fast as it went in, and slows down as it climbs away.
  7. Place your own with B: the first press places the entrance, the second its exit. Each mouth is entered cleanly along the direction you faced when you placed it. Until it has an exit, an entrance leads into itself: you come out of its far side.
Physics choices: each wormhole is a "black hole foil" (Damour & Solodukhin 2007). Outside, its geometry is close to a black hole's; in place of the horizon there is a throat to the other mouth. Light paths are exact for a single wormhole; with several, their effects are combined (an approximation). Clocks at the throat run at 45% of the far-away rate, so the outside looks bluer while you pass through. Lasers stop at a block or after 600 blocks. Balls use game-scaled gravity.
