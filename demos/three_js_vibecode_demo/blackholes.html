What you're seeing

  1. Every pixel is a bent light ray. Rays are traced backwards from your eye along real Schwarzschild light paths, so straight lines behind a hole (the checkerboard, the wall, the pillars) curve around it.
  2. The shadow is about 2.6× the horizon radius, not 1×. Light that comes within 1.5 rs (the photon sphere) spirals in. Just outside it, a thin bright Einstein ring shows a distorted image of the whole scene behind you.
  3. Redshift. Blocks close to a hole look dimmer and redder, because their light loses energy climbing out. Fly close and the outside world turns brighter and bluer instead.
  4. Throw a ball (T) past a hole. It gets stretched along the line to the hole (tides), slows down, reddens and fades at the horizon. That's what a distant observer would actually see, rather than it simply vanishing.
  5. Accretion disk (G): gas orbiting at up to half the speed of light. You can see its far side above and below the shadow, and the side moving toward you is brighter and bluer (Doppler beaming).
  6. Place several holes. Each one lenses the images in the others.
Physics choices: light bending is exact general relativity for a single hole; for several holes their effects are added together (an approximation). Ball gravity is weakened so you can watch balls fall: a real hole this size would weigh about 50 Earths. Tidal stretching of balls is exaggerated for visibility.
