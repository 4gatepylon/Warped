What you're seeing

  1. You're on the inside of a hollow sphere whose inner surface is a mirror. Gravity pulls you outward onto it, so "up" always points at the sun in the centre, and the far side of the world hangs over your head.
  2. The laser reflects every time it reaches the mirror. A beam bouncing around inside a sphere stays in one flat plane through the centre and meets the wall at the same angle every time, so it traces a star-shaped rosette.
  3. It fades as it goes. Its power drops linearly with distance travelled (and by a fixed amount per reflection, if you set a loss). When the power reaches zero the beam stops and nothing more is computed.
  4. Try different heights. Aim along the floor and the beam skims round close to the wall, many short hops (a "whispering gallery"). Jump or fly toward the centre and the hops become long chords right across the sphere. Aim at the sun and it's absorbed.
  5. Everything is ray traced, beam included, so you see the beam, the pillars and the sun reflected again and again in the curved mirror.
  6. Use the jetpack (E). Gravity pulls you outward just as hard everywhere, but the jetpack is stronger: hold it and you speed up toward the centre, fly straight through the middle (and the sun) and come down on the opposite side.
Game rules: a real hollow shell has no gravity inside it (Newton's shell theorem); here the pull is outward with the same strength everywhere (a constant 32 blocks/s², like mg on Earth). The mirror reflects 82% of light, so repeated reflections darken. Light travels in straight lines. Beams stop at pillars, orbs and the sun, and at 256 segments as a safety cap. You can walk through objects and fly through the sun.
